Nova case study

A research system that can say “I don't know.”

Nova is an independent, substantially AI-assisted quantitative research and paper-execution project. No real capital was deployed and no trading edge has been demonstrated.

The problem

Producing a signal was easier than proving the system had earned the right to believe it. Nova was organized around a strict constraint: missing information must not become zero, partial success must not become complete success, and research must not become authorized action without validation.

A governed truth funnel

ingest_raw → clean_raw → enrich → features → regime → story → cards

Provenance and degradation information travel with the data. Risk gates fail closed, execution requires human approval with a matching payload hash, and observability failure becomes unknown rather than silently healthy.

What the audit uncovered

  • Historical bars fetched for calculations but never persisted.
  • Swallowed exceptions recorded as real zero values.
  • UTC/Eastern mismatches selecting plausible but invalid bars.
  • Configuration that did not reach every runtime path.
  • Duplicate classifiers producing incompatible labels.
  • A post-fill error that still appeared successful.

End-to-end evidence

One genuine market observation was traced through 14 unbroken links using real Interactive Brokers market data and paper execution—from ingestion and persistence through risk qualification, operator approval, outcome, learning signal, and evidence consumed by a later decision.

Scope and outcome

At audit time, Nova included approximately 29,000–35,000 lines of Python across approximately 200 modules, approximately 2,900 passing tests, 25 database tables, and 156 REST routes. All 240 recorded orders were paper venue. The strongest demonstrated result is not a profitable strategy; it is a system that preserves negative and insufficient findings without manufacturing confidence.

AI-assisted engineering

AI contributed substantially to implementation. My ownership included problem definition, architecture, requirements, decomposition, invariants, acceptance criteria, implementation review, integration decisions, debugging, testing, and evidence-based capability audits. I do not claim every line was manually written without assistance.
